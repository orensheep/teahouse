HighTable

Web3 needed its community to feel like a membership,
not just a user base.

DeFi protocols speak in the language of APY percentages, liquidity ratios, and wallet addresses. This works for power users — but creates an invisible wall for everyone else.

The brief was open-ended: create something that gives the brand personality, builds loyalty, and drives platform adoption. Our answer was the HighTable — a fictional underground society of elite DeFi operators, drawn from four distinct archetypes.

But rather than simply launching a PFP collection, we designed a complete creative system with a psychological entry mechanic, a visual evolution from mystery to reveal, two tiered membership identities, and a physical brand artefacts — all anchored by a narrative that made ownership feel like belonging.

ART WORK

From Concept to Characters

with Mangaka Artist Amuro

Most collections start with the art and add utility later. HighTable started with the vault — each archetype defined by the strategy tier it unlocks. A Gambler looks that way because a Gambler receives the high-risk vault.

Four archetypes, male and female. A collection premised on this one is you can't offer that to half its audience.

100+ traits make rarity inside an archetype without breaking it. The Zombie type overlays all four — visible rarity, not a metadata percentage.

OG holders co-created their piece with the artist — self-identification that began with a quiz, ending in authorship.

The design didn't arrive complete. It matured with the product — from names on a page, to a world, to a full character system.

PROJECT

Know Your Customers

You can’t email them. You can’t retarget them. So how do you get 5,665 strangers to tell you who they are?

DeFi is pseudonymous by design — no accounts, no email, no analytics. So the community wasn't marketed to. It was built as an instrument: a quiz that makes people classify themselves, a Discord that sorts them, and events aimed at segments the product already knew existed.

WHAT THIS ABILITY ACTUALLY IS

It started as an art brief. It ended as a customer list.

Four archetypes built a world. Gambler, Assassin, Socialite, Hacker — not characters attached to a token, but a society with its own logic, where belonging meant choosing which one you were.

Participation turned visitors into holders. The quiz, the character channels, the whitelist, the dinners — each one something people joined for their own reasons, and each one a step closer to owning a pass.

Holders turned into users. Inside the channels they learned how DeFi actually works and what each strategy does, then went and used Teahouse itself. The NFT was never the destination — it was the route.
