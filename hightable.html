HighTable

A DeFi community that feels like a membership,
not a user base.

DeFi speaks in APY, liquidity ratios and wallet addresses. Power users read it fluently. Everyone else hits a wall.

The brief: give the brand a personality, build loyalty, drive adoption. Our answer was HighTable — a fictional underground society of elite DeFi operators, split into four archetypes.

Not just a PFP drop — a complete creative system: a quiz to get in, a visual arc from mystery to reveal, two membership tiers, and physical brand objects. One story ties it together: owning a pass means belonging.

ART WORK

From Concept to Characters

With manga artist Amuro

Most collections start with art and add utility later. HighTable started with the vaults: each archetype is defined by the strategy tier it unlocks. The Gambler looks the part because it unlocks the high-risk vault.

Four archetypes, each drawn male and female. A collection about belonging can't leave out half its audience.

100+ traits create rarity within each archetype without breaking its look. The Zombie variant spans all four — rarity you can see, not a metadata percentage.

OG holders co-created their piece with the artist. Identity started with a quiz and ended in authorship.

The design grew with the product: from names on a page, to a world, to a full character system.

PROJECT

Know Your Customers

No email. No retargeting. How do you get 5,587 anonymous wallets to tell you who they are?

DeFi is anonymous by design — no accounts, no email, no analytics. So we didn't market to the community; we built it as a research tool: a quiz where people classify themselves, a Discord that sorts them, and events aimed at the segments that revealed.

THE OUTCOME

It started as an art brief. It ended as a customer list.

Four archetypes built a world: Gambler, Assassin, Socialite, Hacker. Not characters on a token, but a society with its own logic — belonging meant choosing which one you are.

Participation turned visitors into holders. The quiz, the channels, the whitelist, the dinners — people joined each for their own reasons, and each was a step closer to owning a pass.

Holders became users. In their channels they learned how DeFi works and what each strategy does, then used Teahouse itself. The NFT wasn't the destination — it was the route.

DeFi is faceless both ways: protocols have no personality, and users are only wallet addresses. The brief was to give the product a face. It also let the product see its users' faces for the first time.
