HighTable

A DeFi community that feels like an exclusive club,
not just a user base.

DeFi is full of complex terms (APY, liquidity ratios, wallet addresses). Pros get it; everyone else gets locked out.

The goal: give the brand a personality, build loyalty, and drive adoption. Our answer: HighTable — a fictional underground society of elite DeFi operators divided into four archetypes.

Not just a profile picture drop, but a complete creative system: an entry quiz, a visual journey from mystery to reveal, two membership tiers, and physical brand merch. One core idea: owning a pass means you belong.

ART WORK

From Concept to Characters

Created with manga artist Amuro

Most NFT projects make the art first and add utility later. HighTable started backward, from the vaults: every character unlocks a specific strategy tier. The Gambler looks risky because it unlocks the high-risk vault.

Four archetypes, each with male and female versions. A collection about belonging cannot leave out half its audience.

Over 100 traits create rarity without ruining the character's look. The Zombie variant spans all four archetypes — rarity you can actually see with your eyes, not just hidden spreadsheet numbers.

OG holders co-created their unique pieces with the artist. Personal identity started with a quick quiz and ended in true collaboration.

The design grew with the product: from words on a page, to a living world, to a full character system.

PROJECT

Know Your Customers

No emails. No retargeting. How do you get 5,587 anonymous wallets to reveal who they are?

DeFi is built to be anonymous — no accounts, no emails, no tracking. So instead of traditional marketing, we built a discovery system: an interactive quiz for self-sorting, a Discord for community grouping, and real-world events tailored to those exact groups.

THE OUTCOME

It started as an art brief. It ended as a real audience list.

Four archetypes built a world: Gambler, Assassin, Socialite, Hacker. Not just icons on a token, but a society with its own rules — belonging meant choosing your archetype.

Participation turned casual visitors into token holders. The quiz, the Discord channels, the whitelist, the dinners — each step gave people a reason to move closer to owning a pass.

Holders became actual product users. Inside their channels, they learned how DeFi works and how each strategy operates, then started using the Teahouse platform. The NFT wasn't the final destination — it was the path to get there.

DeFi is faceless on both ends: protocols have no personality, and users are just wallet addresses. The brief was to give the product a face. In the end, it let the product see its users' faces for the very first time.
