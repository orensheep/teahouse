Teahouse.Finance

It runs on DeFi.
It has to feel like somewhere you'd keep your money.

A vault you can find. A deposit you can finish. A strategy you can actually read.

Teahouse works like a hedge fund you access through a wallet: deposit, let professional strategies run the position, withdraw anytime. Easy to explain, hard to trust. No support line, no chargebacks, no undo — the interface is the only reassurance.

DeFi doesn't lose people on visuals. It loses them on comprehension. My job was to make it usable for one person at a time — not a fund with analysts, but someone depositing a few hundred dollars with no one to ask.

Product UX doesn't decorate the technology. It carries it. Every screen turns what the vaults do into something one person can act on.

USER SEGMENTATION

One engine, two front doors.

Pools vs. Easy Earn

Two products on one engine. Pools came first — for liquidity providers who know the game and just want to know if ours is better.

Easy-Earn came next — for someone holding ETH who just wants it to earn. The vaults are identical. What changes is how much each site explains, and in which words.

Engineering made the flow smooth. The real drop-off was the deposit. My job was to get one person, with one coin, all the way through — without learning anything first.

PRODUCT PLAN

Many vaults, one job: help you rule things out.

Filters narrow by chain, then strategy type. For people filters don't help, Find My Fit asks three questions — none about DeFi. Every row shows the return and its cost: APR, drawdown, AUM and time live.

No screen in this flow is a dead end.

Top row: everything works. One active button at a time, so you always know which step you're on and how many are left.

Bottom row: every way it fails — wrong chain, over balance, rejected signature, no gas, slippage. Each screen says what happened, whether it cost you anything, and what to press next.

PRODUCT PLAN

Same coin, wherever it lives.

Easy-Earn asks one question: which coin do you hold? One card per asset — that's the whole decision.

ETH on Arbitrum is still ETH. Each card covers every chain the asset lives on, so you deposit from wherever your money is — and My Assets shows one total across all chains.

No pairs, no fee tiers, no protocol names. Nothing here needs explaining before you use it.

BRAND DEVELOPMENT

From Leaf Sprout to Teahouse.

The T is a tea leaf sprouting, drawn as one unbroken line on a circle grid.

One stroke, no start, no end. It holds at any size — favicon, sticker, pin, wall — and extends across the family: Easy-Earn, Pools, Tea-REX. Same line, different room.

THE OUTCOME

It started with a customer list. It ended as two products.

HighTable ended with a customer list: four archetypes, each a self-declared risk appetite. This project starts there. Knowing who users are only helps if the product shows them what they're choosing.

So risk stopped being a label and became numbers you can compare: drawdown next to return, fees separate from incentives, how long each vault has actually run. Then the list split in two.

Liquidity providers got Pools — every figure, every filter. Holders got Easy-Earn — one question: which coin do you own? Same engine, same vaults, two front doors, because the right question depends on who's asking. A protocol that couldn't see its users ended up with two products built for the two kinds it found.
