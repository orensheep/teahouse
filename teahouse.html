Teahouse.Finance

It runs on DeFi.
It has to feel like somewhere you'd keep your money.

A vault you can find. A deposit you can finish. A strategy you can actually read.

Teahouse works like a hedge fund you reach through a wallet: you deposit, professional strategies run the position, you withdraw whenever you want. Easy to describe, hard to believe. There's no support line, no chargeback, no undo — the interface is the only reassurance there is.

DeFi doesn't lose people at the visual layer. It loses them at the cognitive one. My job was to make that technology usable by one person at a time: not a fund with analysts, but someone putting in a few hundred dollars with no second opinion.

Product UX doesn't decorate the technology. It carries it. Every screen here exists to turn what the vaults actually do into something one person can act on.

USER SEGMENTATION

One engine, two front doors.

Pools vs. Easy Earn

Two products, one engine underneath. Pools came first. That one's for people who already provide liquidity — they know what they're doing, they just want to know whether ours is better.

Easy-Earn came after, for someone holding some ETH who wants it to earn something. That's as far as their interest goes. The vaults are the same. What changes is how much each site tells you, and which words it uses.

The engineering was support to make the flow smooth. Where people dropped off was the deposit. My job was to get one person, holding one coin, all the way through it — without making them learn anything first.

PRODUCT PLAN

Many vaults, one job: help you rule things out.

Filters narrow from chain to strategy type. Find My Fit handles the people filters don't help — three questions, none about DeFi. Every row shows the return and what it costs: APR, drawdown, AUM, and how long it's been live.

No screen in this flow is a dead end

The top row is what it looks like when everything works — one live button at a time, so you always know which step you're on and how many are left.

The bottom row is every way it doesn't. Wrong chain, over balance, rejected signature, no gas, slippage. Each one names what happened, says whether it cost you anything, and hands back the next thing to press.

PRODUCT PLAN

Same coin, wherever it lives.

Easy-Earn asks one question: which coin do you already hold? One card per asset, and that's the entire decision.

Your ETH doesn't stop being ETH because it sits on Arbitrum. Each card covers every chain the asset lives on — you deposit from wherever your money already is, and My Assets totals it in one number regardless of chain.

No pairs, no fee tiers, no protocol names. Nothing on this screen needs explaining before you can use it.

BRAND DEVELOPMENT

From Leaf Sprout to Teahouse.

The T is a sprout — a tea leaf uncurling, drawn as a single unbroken line on a circle grid.

One stroke, no start and no end. It holds at any size — a favicon, a sticker, a pin, a wall — and it stretches across the whole family: Easy-Earn, Pools, Tea-REX. Same line, different room.

WHAT THIS ABILITY ACTUALLY IS

It started with a customer list. It ended as two products.

HighTable ended with a customer list — four archetypes, each one a self-declared risk appetite. That's where this starts. Knowing who they were only helped if the product could show them what they were choosing.

So risk stopped being a label and became numbers you can compare: drawdown beside return, fees separated from incentives, how long a vault has actually run. Then the list split in two.

People who already provide liquidity got Pools, with every figure and every filter. People who just hold got Easy-Earn, where the only question is which coin you own. Same engine, same vaults, two front doors — because the useful question changes depending on who's asking. A protocol that couldn't see its users ended up with two products shaped around the two kinds it turned out to have.
